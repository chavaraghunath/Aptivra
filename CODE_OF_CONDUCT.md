# Code of Conduct

## The standard

**Blunt about the code. Respectful of the engineer.**

That single line is the whole culture, and both halves are load-bearing. Softening a finding to
spare feelings is a failure of engineering. Sharpening a finding into a personal remark is a failure
of professionalism. Neither is tolerated here.

---

## What we expect

**Critique the work, never the person.** "This does not handle a partial write, and here is the
sequence that loses data" is the review we want. "This is careless" is not — it says nothing
actionable and adds nothing true.

**Bring evidence.** A benchmark, a failing test, a trace, a citation, a line number. "Best practice"
and "other projects do it this way" are not arguments. Neither is seniority.

**Say what you do not know.** "I do not understand this well enough to approve it" is a
contribution, not a failure to contribute.

**Assume competence, then verify.** Ask why before assuming a mistake. Most surprising code has a
reason; some of those reasons are wrong, and asking is how you find out which.

**Receive review as information, not as a verdict on you.** Performative agreement is worse than
disagreement — it hides a defect behind a nod. If you think the reviewer is wrong, say so and show
why. If they are right, fix it and move on. No apology tour is required.

**Report defects immediately, including your own.** A known defect that propagates is an engineering
failure. Stopping the line on your own change is the correct move, not an embarrassment.

**Be honest about completion.** Incomplete work declared complete is a lie, and it is treated as one.
Stubs are promises, not deliverables. Say what you did, what you skipped, and why.

---

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, or demeaning remarks — in any channel, in any form.
- Personal attacks, or criticism aimed at a person rather than at their work.
- Sustained disruption of reviews, discussions, or delivery.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern, a defect, or a vulnerability.
- Weaponising this document to deflect a legitimate technical finding. Rigour is not incivility, and
  a demand for evidence is not an attack.

---

## Scope

This applies to every space connected to the project — repository, pull requests, issues, reviews,
chat, calls, documents, and any setting where you represent this work.

---

## Reporting

If you observe unacceptable behavior, please report it via GitHub Issues or contact the maintainer directly.
Reports are handled confidentially and in good faith.

For **security** vulnerabilities, use private security disclosures — never a public issue.

---

*Correctness is the only authority. Comfort is not a variable — and neither is dignity.*
